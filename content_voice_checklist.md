# Content voice checklist

Use this on **every** public string before it goes on the site: hero, about, experience, customs, automation, case studies, projects, creative, skills, resume, personal, contact, footer, SEO, image alt text, modal headings, and section intros.

Voice: **ClementFranz**, first person, a real person writing his own portfolio. Not a narrator. Not an AI explaining the page.

Hand finished copy to the developer through [content_for_developer.md](content_for_developer.md). Do not dump process notes, file paths, or “how to update the data file” onto the live page.

---

## Run this list every time

1. **First person.** Can I point to *I / me / my* in the sentences that describe work? Labels, nav, buttons, and job titles can stay short. Body copy cannot sound like a bio written about someone else (`Enjoys life with pets`).
2. **Would I say this out loud?** Read it once in a normal speaking voice. If it sounds like a LinkedIn summary, a sales page, or a case-study template, rewrite.
3. **No AI perfume.** Cut: *through-line, leverage, actionable, robust, seamless, streamline, utilize, landscape, delve, I’m passionate, game-changer, unlock, elevate.* Cut slogan couplets (`Smarter X. Faster Y.`). Cut the same triad over and over (`clearer, improved, more reliable`).
4. **No “we hid the AI” energy.** Do not over-prove that a human did this (`I personally built`, `I myself`, `real logistics operations` as a flex). Do not leak the construction of the site (`generic stand-in`, `for portfolio purposes`, `kept secondary so logistics stays the headline`, `not a live client board`, `used as a generic visual`). Just write the work.
5. **No keyword dump.** One sentence should not list every skill I have. Pick the point of that section.
6. **Rhythm.** Mix short and longer sentences. Do not stack em-dashes and three-part lists in every paragraph.
7. **Facts stay facts.** Do not invent metrics, dates, phone numbers, employer names, or client names. Vague results are fine. Fake numbers are not.
8. **Alt text describes the photo.** `Warehouse aisles` is fine. `Warehouse floor used as a generic logistics visual` is not.
9. **Case studies are I-stories.** Challenge can describe the situation. Approach, what I did, and what changed should sound like me talking, not an infinitive brief (`Break the process…`, `Treat compliance…`).
10. **Interests are I-statements**, not LinkedIn hobby captions (`Strategy and quiet problem solving`).
11. **Honest disclaimers stay human.** Customs is operational support, not legal advice. Stock photos can be called stock. Do not wrap those in portfolio-template English.
12. **Positioning holds.** Logistics / operations + automation. Not a generic VA. Design and extra skills can exist without a sentence that explains the content strategy.

If any box fails, rewrite that string. Then run the list again on the rewrite.

## Pass / fail examples

| Fail | Why | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| Enjoys life with pets | Third person | I’ve always liked having animals around. |
| The through-line is practical | Consultant / AI | Same habit every time: learn how the work runs, then cut the extra steps. |
| Smarter visibility. Faster movement. | Slogan | I got tired of people chasing the same shipment status. |
| Supporting photos are stock used for context — not pictures of client work. | Meta, covering tracks | The photos on this page are stock, not shots from client sites. |
| I help teams keep operations organized, shipments moving, and repetitive work under control — with hands-on experience in logistics, customs, procurement, automation, project management, and web technology. | Keyword dump | I work in logistics and operations. I also build the automations that keep that work from turning into endless status checks. |
